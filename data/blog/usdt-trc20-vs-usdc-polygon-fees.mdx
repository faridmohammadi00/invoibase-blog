# USDT-TRC20 vs USDC on Polygon: Fee Reality for Freelancers

When a client pays a crypto invoice, two questions matter more than branding:

1. **How much of the payment survives network fees?**
2. **How reliably can you verify settlement?**

Two popular answers for freelancer invoices are **USDT on TRON (TRC-20)** and **USDC on Polygon**. Here’s a clear comparison.

## Quick comparison

| Factor | USDT-TRC20 (TRON) | USDC on Polygon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typical transfer fee | Often low in absolute USD, varies with energy/bandwidth | Usually cents (or less) when gas is calm |
| Client familiarity | Very high in many markets | High among DeFi / Web3 natives |
| Wallet UX | TronLink / exchange withdrawals common | MetaMask and many multi-chain wallets |
| Settlement speed | Fast finality on TRON | Fast finality on Polygon |
| Accounting clarity | Stablecoin USD peg | Stablecoin USD peg |

Exact fees change with network conditions. Treat the table as an **operating model**, not a fixed price list.

## When freelancers prefer USDT-TRC20

- Clients already hold USDT on TRON (common for cross-border freelance markets)
- Withdrawals from centralized exchanges default to TRC-20
- You want a rail clients already understand

### Watch-outs on TRON

- Confirm the **correct network** before sharing a wallet address
- Account for energy / bandwidth if self-custody wallets are under-resourced
- Keep explorer verification in your receipt workflow

## When freelancers prefer USDC on Polygon

- Clients are already in EVM wallets
- You want cheap, frequent micro-settlements or retainers
- You standardize on Polygon for invoices and treasury

### Watch-outs on Polygon

- Gas spikes are rarer than Ethereum L1, but not impossible
- Confirm the token contract / network in the pay flow
- Bridge friction if a client only holds funds on another chain
